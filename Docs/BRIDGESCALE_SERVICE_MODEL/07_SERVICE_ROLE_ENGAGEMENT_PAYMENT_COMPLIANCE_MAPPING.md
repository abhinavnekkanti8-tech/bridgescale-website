# Service, Role, Engagement, Payment, and Compliance Mapping

## Purpose

This document defines the clean operating model for BridgeScale's diaspora-based fractional sales services.

It separates concepts that should not be mixed:

- service lanes: what BridgeScale sells
- roles: who performs the work
- service templates: what work is done
- engagement types: how the work is structured
- compensation types: how the operator is paid
- payment/compliance requirements: how the engagement can run safely across jurisdictions

This document also reflects two process decisions:

1. Both sides may have a payment gate where applicable. The company pays to unblur matches. The operator/talent may also pay to activate or unlock participation depending on the final platform fee model.
2. The Master Services Agreement is signed before the SOW is shared with both parties for final engagement review and signature.

## Corrected Marketplace Flow

Recommended flow:

1. Company completes intake.
2. BridgeScale generates a gap / need / seeking diagnosis using templates and/or LLM-assisted drafting.
3. Matches are generated.
4. Company sees blurred match previews.
5. Company pays unlock fee to unblur matches.
6. Operator/talent payment gate is satisfied where applicable.
   - Example: talent active-pool unlock, platform activation, or paid-tier access.
   - This should be transparent and not hidden inside payout deductions.
7. Company requests or accepts a 30-minute call.
8. Operator accepts the 30-minute call.
9. 30-minute call happens.
10. Both sides indicate engagement intent.
11. BridgeScale shares a pre-SOW commercial summary.
12. BridgeScale generates or retrieves the Master Services Agreement for the company/operator pair.
13. MSA is signed by the required parties before full SOW is shared for final approval.
14. BridgeScale creates a draft SOW from the relevant service template.
15. SOW is reviewed, edited, and approved.
16. Company and operator sign SOW.
17. Invoice/payment schedule starts according to the SOW.
18. Engagement begins.

### Engagement Types

Engagement type is how the work is structured.

| Engagement Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Consultation | One short session, usually 30-90 minutes, with a specific question or diagnosis objective |
| Sprint | Time-boxed work package, usually 30-60 days |
| Retainer | Ongoing fractional work, usually monthly with a minimum term |
| Fractional Leadership Engagement | Senior operator embedded in leadership rhythm, usually 90 days or longer |
| Advisory / Equity Engagement | Advice and strategic support compensated partly or entirely through equity |
| Success-Fee Engagement | Work where compensation is partly or fully tied to a defined outcome |

## Payment and Compliance Requirements

### Required Fields Per Engagement

Every BridgeScale engagement should capture these fields.

| Field | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| serviceLane | Controls positioning and role pool |
| roleType | Controls matching and screening |
| serviceTemplate | Controls SOW template and deliverables |
| engagementType | Controls term, cancellation, and workflow |
| compensationType | Controls invoice, payout, tax, and addenda |
| paymentSchedule | Controls invoice timing and operator payout timing |
| companyCountry | Determines collection/payment provider and tax context |
| operatorCountry | Determines tax form, contractor/EOR/AOR risk, and payout method |
| payerEntity | Entity paying BridgeScale |
| payoutEntity | Entity receiving payout from BridgeScale/payment partner |
| taxFormRequired | W-9, W-8BEN, local equivalent, or partner-managed |
| complianceMode | Contractor, AOR/COR, EOR, direct employment, or legal review |
| EORorAORRequired | Whether vendor partner is required |
| successFeeTrigger | Defines outcome event if success-fee is used |
| equityDocsRequired | Defines whether FAST/equity docs/addendum are required |

### Compliance Mode Definitions

| Mode | Use When |
|---|---|
| Contractor | Short-term, independent, output-based work with low control by company |
| AOR / COR | Contractor relationship exists but classification, onboarding, tax collection, and payout should be managed by a vendor |
| EOR | Relationship looks employment-like, or local law makes contractor classification risky |
| Direct Employment | Full-time conversion or direct hire by company |
| Legal Review Required | Equity, success fee, regulated market, sensitive data, or ambiguous worker classification |

### Common Tax Forms / Documents

| Operator Country / Status | Common Requirement |
|---|---|
| US person / US contractor | W-9; 1099 workflow may apply if BridgeScale/payment partner is payer |
| Non-US individual paid by US entity | W-8BEN commonly used to certify foreign status |
| Non-US entity paid by US entity | W-8BEN-E or local equivalent may apply |
| India-based operator | local tax/GST/TDS review may apply depending entity and payer |
| EU/UK operator | local contractor classification, VAT, invoice, and employment-risk review may apply |
| EOR engagement | EOR partner handles employment paperwork, payroll, local taxes, and statutory obligations |

Note: final tax treatment must be reviewed by legal/finance. BridgeScale should avoid promising tax advice.

## Tax and Payout Data Collection Timing

BridgeScale should not require full W-9, W-8BEN, W-8BEN-E, KYC, GST/PAN, or partner onboarding before an operator can merely enter the matching experience.

That creates unnecessary drop-off.

### Recommended Timing

| Stage | Collect |
|---|---|
| Operator signup | country of residence, tax residency, preferred payout country, preferred payout currency, individual vs entity |
| Profile completion | whether operator can receive payouts in current country, whether operator has business entity, whether operator wants India payout |
| Before entering active paid engagement | required tax form path, KYC/KYB, bank details, entity documents, partner onboarding if needed |
| Before payout | final bank verification, tax form completion, invoice/payment documentation |

### Early Fields to Add

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| countryOfResidence | Worker classification and tax context |
| taxResidencyCountry | Tax-document routing |
| payoutCountry | Determines payout rail |
| payoutCurrency | USD, INR, EUR, GBP, etc. |
| payoutAccountType | personal, business, NRE, NRO, savings, current |
| individualOrEntity | Individual contractor vs company/LLP |
| indiaPayoutRequested | Flags Indian payout flow |
| hasIndianPAN | Needed only if India tax flow applies |
| hasGSTRegistration | Needed only if Indian entity/export services flow applies |

### Principle

Collect enough early to route matches and compliance risk. Collect full tax/KYC documents only when money is likely to move.

## Two-Sided Platform Fee Model

BridgeScale needs to decide whether it charges one side or both sides.

### Option A: Company-Side Only

Company pays operator fee plus BridgeScale platform fee.

Pros:

- simplest to explain
- strongest talent trust
- operator sees stated compensation

Cons:

- company-facing price is higher
- less marketplace-style two-sided monetization

### Option B: Two-Sided 70/30 Platform Fee Split

BridgeScale charges a total platform fee split between company and talent.

Example if total platform fee is 10% on a USD 5,000 operator fee:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Operator stated fee | USD 5,000 |
| Company-side fee, 7% | USD 350 |
| Talent-side fee, 3% | USD 150 |
| Company pays | USD 5,350 |
| Operator receives | USD 4,850 |
| BridgeScale earns before processing/vendor costs | USD 500 |

Pros:

- platform monetizes both sides
- company-facing markup is lower than full 10%

Cons:

- operators may dislike deductions
- requires very clear payout disclosure
- requires payout statements showing gross fee, platform fee, processing/vendor fees, and net payout

### Option C: Hybrid Fee Model

Company pays service platform fee. Talent pays separate subscription/activation fee, not a per-engagement deduction.

Pros:

- avoids surprise payout deductions
- keeps two-sided monetization

Cons:

- more complicated product/pricing design
- must explain value of talent fee clearly

## Recommended Disclosure Copy

If company-side only:

> BridgeScale charges a platform fee to the company on paid engagements. This fee does not reduce the operator's stated compensation unless the SOW says otherwise.

If two-sided:

> BridgeScale charges a transparent platform fee on both sides of paid engagements. The company-side fee is shown before payment. The operator-side fee is shown before the operator accepts the engagement and appears on the payout statement.

## Open Decisions

These must be resolved before implementation:

1. Is the platform fee company-side only, two-sided, or subscription plus company-side fee?
2. Is talent payment a one-time activation, recurring subscription, per-engagement deduction, or some combination?
